Not all e-waste is equal. A kilo of plastic casings is worth very little. A kilo of processors or plated connectors can be worth a lot. If your company generates electronic scrap, knowing which parts hold precious metals helps you **separate high-value material** and get a fair price.

## 1. Integrated circuits (ICs)

ICs contain very fine **gold bonding wires** that connect the silicon chip to its pins. Some also have gold- or silver-plated leads.

ICs come from almost every device: computers, TVs, radar and telecom equipment, microcontrollers, memory devices, audio and microwave amplifiers, voltage regulators, calculators, clocks, timers, counters and temperature sensors.

**Tip:** ceramic ICs and older processors usually carry more gold than modern plastic chips. Keep them separate. [See plastic ICs](/materials/#plastic-ics).

## 2. Processors (CPUs)

Older ceramic processors, especially from the 1990s and early 2000s, have gold-plated lids and pins. They are among the richest single items in e-scrap.

## 3. Circuit boards (PCBs)

Board value depends on type:

- **High grade:** telecom boards, server boards, boards with many ICs and gold edge connectors.
- **Medium grade:** desktop motherboards, network cards.
- **Low grade:** power supply boards, TV boards with few chips.

## 4. Memory cards and RAM

SD and microSD cards have **gold-plated contacts** and memory chips. Bulk lots of old or rejected cards from cameras, smartphones, drones and game consoles are a good source. [See memory cards](/materials/#memory-cards).

## 5. Connectors, pins and contacts

Connectors, edge fingers, relay contacts and switch contacts are often plated with **gold or silver** to prevent corrosion. They are processed by solvent extraction without needing thermal treatment.

## 6. Thin films

Flexible thin films appear in aerospace parts, consumer devices, medical devices, battery backup devices and energy harvesting systems. Many use precious-metal layers. [See thin films](/materials/#thin-films).

## 7. Capacitors and sensors

Some multilayer ceramic capacitors contain **palladium and silver**. Certain sensors and electrodes, including in medical devices, contain **platinum**.

## 8. Rags, wipes and sweeps

Not an electronic part, but worth mentioning: rags, wipes, gloves and filters from electronics assembly and plating lines collect fine precious-metal dust. They can be processed by thermal reduction.

## How to get the most value

1. **Sort before you sell.** Keep ICs, CPUs, memory and high-grade boards away from low-grade scrap.
2. **Keep it dry and in closed bags or boxes.**
3. **Note the quantity and take photos.**
4. **Work with a recycler who recovers precious metals**, not just a general scrap buyer.
