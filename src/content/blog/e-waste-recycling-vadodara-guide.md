Vadodara (Baroda) is one of Gujarat’s largest industrial cities. Engineering firms, chemical and pharma plants, IT offices, hospitals and colleges all produce electronic waste. This guide explains how a business in Vadodara can deal with it the right way, and why it can even be worth money.

## What counts as e-waste?

E-waste is any electrical or electronic equipment you no longer use, plus its parts. For a typical Vadodara business, that includes:

- Old desktops, laptops, servers and networking equipment
- Printed circuit boards (PCBs) and rejected electronic assemblies
- Integrated circuits (ICs), memory chips and memory cards
- Monitors, TVs and screens
- Control panels, drives and PLCs from factory machines
- UPS units, LED lighting and electrical panels
- Electronic medical and lab equipment

If it has a plug, a battery or a circuit board, it is almost certainly e-waste.

## Why you should not sell it to the local kabadi

Many companies still hand old electronics to informal scrap dealers. It feels easy, but it causes three problems:

1. **Pollution.** Informal workers often burn cables and boards in the open or use acid baths to pull out metals. This releases toxic fumes and chemicals near homes and farms.
2. **Compliance risk.** Under India’s [E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022](/blog/e-waste-management-rules-2022-gujarat/), e-waste should go to proper, registered channels. Handing it to an unknown dealer leaves no paper trail.
3. **Lost value.** Circuit boards and chips contain gold, silver, palladium and copper. A generic scrap buyer usually prices them as mixed scrap, not for their precious-metal content.

## The hidden value in your e-waste

A tonne of gold ore gives about 5 grams of gold. A tonne of mobile phone circuit boards can give around 150 grams, roughly 30 times more. This is why e-waste is called the **urban mine**.

For a factory or IT office, this means old boards, ICs and memory are not just waste to dispose of. They are a material with real metal value. [Read how gold is recovered from e-waste](/blog/how-gold-is-recovered-from-e-waste/).

## How to choose an e-waste recycler in Vadodara

Ask these questions before you hand over any material:

- **Where is the material processed?** A local plant means shorter transport and easier visits.
- **What happens to it?** Ask the recycler to explain their process in simple words.
- **How is pollution controlled?** Thermal processes should have proper exhaust treatment, such as an afterburner and a wet scrubber.
- **Do they value precious metals?** A recycler that recovers precious metals can price boards and chips fairly.
- **Will you get documents?** Keep a record of what you handed over, when, and to whom.
