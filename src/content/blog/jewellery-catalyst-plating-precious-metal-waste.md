When people think of recycling precious metals, they think of old phones and computers. But some of the richest sources are not electronic at all. Gujarat, with its jewellery hubs, chemical belts and plating units, has plenty of them.

## Jewellery industry waste

Surat and Rajkot are among India’s best-known jewellery and diamond centres, and there are many workshops in Ahmedabad and Vadodara too. Every workshop loses a small amount of metal every day into:

- Floor sweeps and bench dust
- Polishing and buffing waste
- Rags, wipes and gloves
- Water and air filters
- Crucibles and melting residues

Each item looks worthless, but over months the gold and silver add up. **Thermal reduction** burns off the organic material and leaves a metal-rich ash, which is blended so its gold and silver content can be measured accurately. ## Spent catalysts

The chemical and pharmaceutical plants around **Vadodara, Bharuch, Ankleshwar and Dahej** use catalysts to speed up reactions. Many contain **platinum, palladium or rhodium**. When a catalyst is spent, those metals are still there. These metals are rare and not renewable, so recovering them locally matters. [See spent catalyst recovery](/services/spent-catalyst-recovery/).

## Plating waste

Electroplating units and electronics makers produce plating solutions waste, plated rejects and off-cuts containing gold and silver. Solvent extraction recovers metal from surface-plated items. [See plating & manufacturing waste](/services/plating-waste-recovery/).

## Tips for getting the most value

1. **Collect consistently.** Use dedicated bins for sweeps, rags and filters.
2. **Keep streams separate.** Don’t mix gold-bearing jewellery waste with general trash.
3. **Keep it dry and sealed.**
4. **Work with a recycler whose process can handle low-grade material.** Our systems are engineered to work efficiently even at low volumes and low metal content.
